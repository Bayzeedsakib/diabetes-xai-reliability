## 1. Research question and hypotheses (current rules; D33 amendment items 6–9, D35)
RQ: On BRFSS 2015, when several models from different families are near-equivalent on AP, do their SHAP explanations agree beyond what refitting alone changes, and how reliable are LIME explanations relative to SHAP?

| ID | Hypothesis (pre-stated) | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | At least two models from different families are near-equivalent to the top model on AP. | Top model of a split = highest mean development AP in that split (D7 rule). Model m is equivalent to it if the 90% paired-bootstrap CI of ΔAP (test) lies in (−δ, δ), δ = 0.01 (TOST, α = 0.05). The TOST p-value of each candidate (max of the two one-sided paired-bootstrap p-values) is Holm-adjusted across the 5 candidates per split, because "at least one other-family member" is a union of claims. Near-equivalent set = top model + equivalent models. H1 holds if the set contains ≥ 2 families in ≥ 4 of 5 splits. Families: boosting (XGB, LGBM, CAT), bagging (RF), linear (LR), neural (MLP). Sensitivity: δ = 0.005 and 0.02. Across splits: mean ± SD of ΔAP and a TOST with the Nadeau–Bengio corrected variance (1/J + n_test/n_train = 1/5 + 0.25). δ was set after the split-1 (thesis) results were known; the paper says so. |
| H2 | The members share the top-5 SHAP set, and rank-weighted agreement is higher within the boosting family than across families. | Identical top-5 set for all member pairs in ≥ 4 of 5 splits (two 5-sets give Jaccard 1.0 or ≤ 0.667). RBO (p = 0.8): mean within-family > mean cross-family in ≥ 4 of 5 splits. Fallback: if the set has one family only, the within- vs cross-family comparison is computed over all six models and labelled exploratory. |
| H3 | Disagreement between members exceeds within-model refit disagreement. | Refit-level distributions: within = RBO(refit i of A, refit j of A), i ≠ j; between = RBO(refit i of A, refit j of B). Statistic T = min(median within A, median within B) − median between. A pair passes in a split if the median between-RBO is below both within-medians AND the one-sided permutation p < 0.05 (model labels permuted among the pooled refits of A and B; p = share of label assignments with T ≥ T_obs, the observed assignment included). With 5 + 5 refits there are C(10, 5) = 252 assignments; swapping the A and B labels gives the same T, so they form 126 distinct values and the minimum p is 2/252 ≈ 0.008. Split 1 (10 + 10 refits): 10,000 sampled assignments. H3 holds for a pair if it passes in ≥ 4 of 5 splits. Reported in native and in common explainer settings. |
| H4 | SHAP–LIME agree on sign but not on rank, and LIME's rank agreement depends more on its settings than on its seed. | Sign: median over cases × models of the sign agreement on SHAP's top 5 (seed-mean LIME weights, reference setting T5) ≥ 0.95. Rank: per case × model, SD_set = mean over the 3 seeds of the SD (ddof 1) of the SHAP–LIME RBO (p = 0.8) across the 11 grid settings; SD_seed = mean over the 11 settings of the SD across the 3 seeds; supported if SD_set > SD_seed for more than half of the case × model pairs and the one-sided exact sign test gives p < 0.05. Sensitivity: the 9 new settings only; τ-b instead of RBO (section 10, item 6). |
| H5 | The CholCheck gap between SHAP and LIME is a scaling effect of LIME's "same value" representation, not a difference in reference distribution (section 9 item 9). | share = |attribution of CholCheck| / Σ|attributions| per case × model; gap = |median LIME share − median SHAP share|. Decided on (a) rescaled LIME (every binary-predictor weight × |x_j − p_j|, p_j = development share of 1s; no new runs): supported if gap_a ≤ 0.5 × gap_native. (b) SHAP with a CholCheck 50/50 background and (c) LIME with CholCheck sampled 50/50 are reported separately; predictions of the account in section 10, item 7. |
| E1 (exploratory) | The SHAP–LIME rank gap grows with predictor imbalance. | Rank gap = median over cases of (rank by |SHAP| − rank by |LIME|) per predictor × model; imbalance = mean over cases of LIME's training frequency of the case's own value or bin; Spearman over 21 predictors × 6 models and over the 14 binary predictors; no confirmatory claim. |
| E2 (exploratory) | Respondents who reported prediabetes (coded 0) get higher predicted risk than other negatives. | Within test negatives: ROC-AUC of the model score for prediabetes (910) vs no diabetes (42,757); frozen thesis models, split 1; prevalence control (section 10, item 4). |

## 2. Data and splits
- File: diabetes_binary_health_indicators_BRFSS2015.csv (SHA-256 19f367e3…d0); duplicates kept and split at row level (D2).
- Five outer stratified 80:20 splits:
  - Split 1 is the thesis partition; its Stage 3–6 outputs for LR, RF, XGB, LGBM, CAT and MLP are reused.
  - Splits 2–5 use seeds from a new 15th stream, paper_outer, appended to S2 (prefix-stable, so no existing seed changes). Per-split seeds come from SeedSequence([paper_outer, k]) for the partition, folds, the 50k subsample, the SHAP background (500 dev rows), the permutation background (100, a subset of the 500) and the test core (500 rows, 70 positives).
  - Index files are frozen before any model is fitted (S1, S4 rules). Stratified samples use the Stage 1 procedure (section 10, item 5).
- Models: LR, RF, XGB, LGBM, CAT, MLP. CatBoost always with thread_count = 12 (section 10, item 1).
- Tuning per split: the same 30 frozen candidate configurations per model (stage2 search_configs), 5-fold CV on that split's development data, AP scoring, refit on full development data. MLP is tuned on the split's 50k subsample and refitted on full development data (D14). Estimator seed 22980462 (S7(5)).
- Thresholds: MCC-max and screening (sensitivity ≥ 0.80) from pooled OOF probabilities (S12c).
- Test evaluation once per split: AP, ROC-AUC, Brier, and sensitivity/specificity/MCC at the MCC threshold; 2,000 paired class-stratified bootstrap resamples (seed per split).

## 3. Explanations
- Comparison set (student proposal accepted). Within a split, every model and refit is explained on that split's own 500-row test core; local comparisons stay within a split. Across splits, only global rankings are compared, as population-level summaries. A fixed explanation set across splits is not feasible: about 0.2⁵ × 253,680 ≈ 80 rows are test rows in all five random splits.
- Native setting (as in the thesis):
  - Tree: interventional TreeSHAP with the 500-row background (RF probability; XGB, LGBM, CAT log-odds).
  - Linear: LinearExplainer for LR (log-odds).
  - Permutation: PermutationExplainer for MLP (100-row background, max_evals 250).
- Common setting (confound ablation, student item 5). PermutationExplainer for all six models on predict_proba, with the same 100-row background and max_evals 250. This fixes explainer type, background (size and rows) and output scale together. In split 1 the approximation error is separated from the setting effect for XGB (section 9 item 1).
- Agreement measures: top-3 and top-5 Jaccard; RBO on the complete 21-predictor rankings, p = 0.8 (sensitivity p = 0.9); Kendall τ-b; sign agreement. τ-b uses average ranks for ties, as in the thesis; rankings for Jaccard and RBO break ties by feature order (stable sort).
- Refit stability: 10 bootstrap refits of the development set in split 1, 5 in splits 2–5 (within-class resampling with replacement; tuned hyperparameters fixed; native setting; same core and background). Secondary row: 5 seed-only refits for the stochastic models in split 1 (LR is unchanged by the seed).
- Within-family Rashomon set (split 1 only): from the existing Stage 3 cv_results, all configurations within 0.005 AP of each model's best mean CV AP, up to 10 per model, refitted and explained on the core. Laberge et al. (JMLR 2023) and Rudin et al. (ICML 2024) are cited for this set only; the cross-family group is called the near-equivalent set.
- SHAP vs LIME (split 1, R2): cases = the 500 core rows + the 30-case thesis panel; settings as in section 10, item 6 (11 grid settings + one CholCheck intervention); 3 seeds each; measures: sign agreement, top-5 Jaccard, RBO, τ-b, LIME R², local gap, seed stability.
- CholCheck test (H5): (b) SHAP with a CholCheck 50/50 background; (c) LIME with CholCheck sampled 50/50; plus (a) rescaled LIME (section 9 item 9). The interventions were run for CholCheck only (section 10, item 8).

## 5. Statistics (current rules)
- Within split: paired bootstrap (B = 2,000) CIs; TOST on the 90% paired-bootstrap CI.
- Multiplicity: H1 Holm-adjusts the TOST p-values across the 5 candidates per split (section 1); H3 uses the permutation test with the pass rule of section 1; no other adjustment.
- Across splits: mean ± SD (ddof 1) and the Nadeau–Bengio corrected resampled t-test for named differences (e.g., CAT − LR AP).
- Confirmatory family = H1–H5 with the decision rules of section 1. Everything else is exploratory.
- Negative outcomes are reported as such; e.g., if H1 fails, the paper states that only one family is near-equivalent.
